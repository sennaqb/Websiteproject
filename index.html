The Descent

Between the surface and the deepest point on Earth lie 10,935 meters of water. Scroll to sink.

0–200m

Zone I · Epipelagic

The sunlight zone

Sunlight still reaches this thin top layer, so plants can grow here. Drifting phytoplankton make roughly half of the oxygen you breathe.

Light fades fast. Red goes first, within the top few meters, then orange and yellow. By 200 meters only about one percent of surface light is left, and it is all blue.

200–1,000m

Zone II · Mesopelagic

The twilight zone

Every night, billions of animals rise from here to feed near the surface, then sink back before dawn. It is the largest migration on the planet, and it happens every day.

Too dark for plants, still too bright to hide in. Animals here use counter-illumination: lights on their bellies that match the faint glow from above and erase their silhouettes.

1,000–4,000m

Zone III · Bathypelagic

The midnight zone

76%

of animals in the deep water column make their own light.

No sunlight reaches this deep. The only light down here is made by living things.

Female anglerfish dangle a lure lit by glowing bacteria. Dragonfish shine red light, a color most deep animals cannot see, and use it like a private searchlight.

Martini & Haddock, Scientific Reports, 2017. Monterey Bay, surface to 3,900 m.

4,000–6,000m

Zone IV · Abyssopelagic

The abyss

Near freezing and completely dark. The flat abyssal plains down here cover about half of Earth's surface, more than all the continents combined.

6,000–10,935m

Zone V · Hadalpelagic

The trenches

Named for Hades. Only the trenches cut this deep, where one plate of Earth's crust slides beneath another.

Fish give out a little past 8,000 meters. Below that, the bottom belongs to amphipods, sea cucumbers and microbes living on what drifts down from above.
